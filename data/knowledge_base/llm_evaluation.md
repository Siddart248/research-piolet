# LLM System Evaluation and Guardrails

Evaluating generative AI systems requires assessing stochastic, natural-language outputs across accuracy, safety, and reasoning consistency.

## The RAG Evaluation Triad

In Retrieval-Augmented Generation architectures, evaluation centers around three distinct dimensions:
1. **Context Relevance**: Measures whether retrieved chunks are pertinent to the user's question without extraneous noise.
2. **Faithfulness / Groundedness**: Evaluates whether every claim in the generated answer can be directly inferred from the retrieved source context, detecting ungrounded hallucinations.
3. **Answer Relevance**: Checks whether the generated response directly and completely answers the user's inquiry, irrespective of length.

## Evaluation Methodologies

- **Deterministic Unit Assertions**: Checking structured output adherence, JSON schema validity, required key presence, regex constraints, and latency thresholds.
- **LLM-as-a-Judge**: Prompting a capable model (such as Gemini 2.5 Flash or Pro) with a structured rubric to score answers on a scale (e.g., 1-5) or binary flag (`is_grounded: true/false`).
- **Offline Golden Datasets**: Curation of representative test queries with ground-truth expected contexts and target answers (`evaluation/test_cases.json`).

## Pitfalls of LLM-as-a-Judge

- **Verbosity Bias**: Judges often reward longer, verbose answers over concise correct ones.
- **Position Bias**: Preference for the first or last candidate in comparative side-by-side rankings.
- **Self-Preference**: Models tend to rate answers generated by their own family or architecture higher than those from competing providers.
